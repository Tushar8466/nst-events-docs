## 1. Runtime Policy

* **Node.js**: The repository will always target the Active LTS (Long Term Support) release of Node.js.
* **Upgrades**: Migration to a new LTS version occurs no earlier than 30 days after its official LTS designation, allowing the broader ecosystem to stabilize.

## 2. Upgrade Cadences

Dependencies must be categorized and updated according to specific cadences to balance stability with security.

### Patch Upgrades (x.y.Z)
* **Cadence**: Continuous / Automated.
* **Rule**: Patch updates (bug fixes) should be merged automatically provided they pass all Tier B Quality Gates.

### Minor Upgrades (x.Y.z)
* **Cadence**: Monthly.
* **Rule**: Minor updates (new features, non-breaking) are batched and reviewed monthly to ensure they do not introduce subtle behavioral changes or bloat.

### Major Upgrades (X.y.z)
* **Cadence**: Quarterly or as required.
* **Rule**: Major updates (breaking changes) require dedicated engineering tasks. They must not be bundled with feature work. An engineer must audit the library's changelog, update our implementation to match the new APIs, and ensure all tests pass.

## 3. Security Updates

Vulnerability reports generated by the [Security Pipeline (ENG-006)](./05-security-pipeline.md) supersede standard cadences.
* **Critical / High Severity**: Must be patched and deployed within 24 hours of discovery. If a patch is unavailable, the dependency must be mitigated or removed.
* **Medium / Low Severity**: Evaluated and batched into the next scheduled deployment.

## 4. Dependency Ownership

Every third-party dependency introduces risk.
* **Addition Policy**: Before adding a new dependency, engineers must evaluate it for active maintenance, bundle size, license compatibility, and lack of overlap with existing packages.
* **Approval**: Introducing a new top-level dependency requires approval from a Principal Architect during the Pull Request review.
